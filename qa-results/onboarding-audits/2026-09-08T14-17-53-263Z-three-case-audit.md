## Human visual review of all nine candidates

The six desktop and six mobile candidate screenshots were reviewed together. These findings supplement the deterministic checks and remain separate from the unavailable AI visual review.

### Critical and high-priority visible problems

1. **Owned therapist photography is retained but invisible.** All three rich-case manifests claim two explicit placements and award imagery a score of 95, yet the hero and supporting image areas render blank. The production media upload path is stored in a different URL shape from imported object assets, and the generation/renderer pipeline does not resolve it before placing it. A customer who supplied carefully chosen photos would see a generic, empty site.
2. **Mobile direction 3 clips horizontally.** In the rich and sparse cases, headings and sections begin outside the left edge or collapse into extremely narrow text columns. This is a release-blocking responsive defect, not a cosmetic warning.
3. **Imported copy is effectively unedited.** Long source passages are poured into narrow sections as walls of tiny text, especially on mobile. The result is unusable even though the retained quality score is 88.
4. **The three choices are mostly one fallback template with reordered blocks.** Across all cases, they share the same pale surfaces, green CTA panel, generic headings, section vocabulary, and overall rhythm. Manifest labels such as editorial, organic, and structured overstate the visible difference.
5. **Sparse onboarding does not produce a custom illustration system.** The dossier explicitly asked for an illustration direction without pretending assets exist. The manifests describe organic fields or geometric lines, but the screenshots show mostly empty white/beige blocks and occasional broken-image placeholders.
6. **Unsupported generic claims leak into customer copy.** The rich case invents trust/experience language despite explicit prohibitions; the sparse case also triggers prohibited-claim checks. Internal customer instructions are copied into visible timeline content instead of being transformed into customer-facing prose.
7. **Direction scores are not calibrated to evidence.** Candidates receive 84–88 overall, 95–100 imagery, and 95 responsiveness while images are blank, mobile layouts clip, content quality is 52, and visual review never ran. These scores should not be shown as confidence.
8. **No decision workspace or safe selection proof exists.** All runs are fallback/not-ready, so the direction cards are absent and selection correctly returns 409. Candidate preview fingerprints also fail to match retained fingerprints, so preview parity is unproven.
9. **Requested booking is only visual language.** The therapist and imported sites say “book,” but no native booking component, owned services, or selectable slots exist. The calls to action therefore promise a flow that is not present.
10. **Pages are structurally thin.** About and contact pages contain a single meaningful section. The rich case asks for four pages but receives three; service details are repeated as dense paragraphs rather than structured offerings.
11. **Imported imagery lacks responsive art direction.** Some source images render, but the same placements and generic crop descriptions are repeated across directions. There is no visible focal-point strategy or meaningful mobile crop variation.
12. **Typography and whitespace are uncontrolled.** Several pages have enormous empty vertical regions, weak content density, tiny imported body text, and insufficient hierarchy. The fallback renderer is technically producing pages, not polished customer-ready sites.

### Audit-harness finding

The sparse case was incorrectly asked to provide booking screenshots even though booking was explicitly not requested. This did not cause its terminal result—the quality gate already failed—but future runs now mark that evidence not applicable.

## Cross-system image and illustration roadmap

1. **Ask for intent, not just files** — capture subject, consent/ownership, intended page role, emotional purpose, must-keep details, and whether the customer accepts a generated alternative.
2. **Analyze before placement** — record orientation, resolution, focal point, faces, visual quality, dominant colors, negative space, crop safety, and hero suitability for every owned/imported image.
3. **Plan a site-wide sequence** — allocate each image once across hero, practitioner story, environment, services and supporting moments; reject accidental repetition and avoid decorative images that add no meaning.
4. **Store responsive art direction** — retain separate desktop and mobile crop/focal-point settings rather than applying one `background-position` everywhere.
5. **Make illustrations genuinely custom** — choose one explicit system per direction (for example organic line work, geometric editorial shapes, or tactile collage), with palette, stroke, texture, corner language and icon rules tied to the brand.
6. **Separate generated, stock and owned media** — label provenance visibly, never imply a generated person/place is the real practitioner or clinic, and require customer approval before publishing synthetic documentary-style imagery.
7. **Expose customer controls** — let customers replace an asset, choose focal point, adjust mobile crop, hide an image, select illustration intensity, and request a different visual without regenerating the whole site.
8. **Fail helpfully** — when no suitable image exists, use typography and custom decorative illustration intentionally; show a concrete shot list instead of silently inserting generic stock.

## Retention and honesty

- All run IDs, synthetic identities, websites, candidate fingerprints, screenshots and failure states remain in the retained manifest and database.
- `PASSED` is only possible when generation is ready, deterministic checks pass, all three candidates exist, six candidate screenshots exist, two decision screenshots exist, and every candidate has visual-review evidence.
- Booking, provider, import and database failures are reported separately and are never converted into visual success.
